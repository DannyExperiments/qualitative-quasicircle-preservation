# Q1722: qualitative quasicircle preservation implies quasiconformality

## Exact source and answer

Booth, Nolte and Verberne, *Graphs of Quasicircles and Quasiconformal Homeomorphisms*, [arXiv:2601.09892v1, §1, printed and PDF p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09892v1#page=2), ask:

> Is every homeomorphism of a closed surface that sends quasicircles to quasicircles quasiconformal?

See the [pinned catalog entry](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/computer_science_ai/classical-statistical-machine-learning.md#q1722). The authors' graph theorem concerns closed oriented surfaces of genus at least two and allows orientation-reversing quasiconformal maps.

**Theorem.** Yes. On any closed oriented Riemann surface, a homeomorphism taking every quasicircle to a quasicircle is quasiconformal. On a surface of positive genus, the same conclusion holds when only essential quasicircles are tested. In either formulation the individual image constants may be unrelated and the inverse need not be assumed to preserve quasicircles.

Use the two-point bounded turning characterization of quasicircles: the smaller of the diameters of the two subarcs between any two points must be at most a fixed multiple of their distance. For the authors' essential curves, this is [§2.2, Remark 1 and Lemma 2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09892v1#S2.SS2), where the constant need not be the same as the analytic dilatation. All smooth metric choices on the closed surface are bilipschitz equivalent. For a sphere one may use the classical Ahlfors characterization. In the proof, a sufficiently small conformal coordinate disk with compact closure has Euclidean and surface distances comparable on a smaller closed disk.

## Proof

**Distinct bad centers.** If f is not quasiconformal, choose conformal charts in source and target and a relatively compact open set W of the source chart, with f(closure W) in the target chart, on which the coordinate map F is not quasiconformal. Such relatively compact sets cover the compact surface, and a finite cover would otherwise give a global bound. Put H(z)=limsup as r→0 of max_{|u-z|=r}|F(u)-F(z)| divided by min_{|u-z|=r}|F(u)-F(z)|. For every M the set {z∈W:H(z)>M} is infinite. If it were finite, the metric characterization would give quasiconformality with a uniform constant on its complement in W. Finite points are removable here. In the modulus characterization, the families of curves hitting the finite set or its image have modulus zero. At each puncture a small multiple of 1/(r log(1/r)) has arbitrarily small L² norm but infinite line integral along any nonconstant rectifiable curve reaching the puncture. Modulus comparisons on the punctured domains thus extend across the points.

Select distinct centers z_j with H(z_j)→∞ converging to z_* in closure W. Discard any center equal to z_*. Rotate and translate the source coordinates and take a subsequence so z_*=(0,0), z_j=(t_j,y_j), t_j>0, |y_j|≤t_j/4 and t_{j+1}<t_j/4. At each center choose r_j<t_j/100 as small as necessary for corresponding circles to have max/min distortion tending to infinity. The radius can also be chosen to place every ball B(z_j,8r_j) in a fixed small chart rectangle about zero.

**Separated triples.** There are two points a_j,b_j on the circle of radius r_j with angle at z_j in [π/4,3π/4] and with |F(a_j)-F(z_j)|/|F(b_j)-F(z_j)|→∞. Start with a maximum and a minimum. If their angle is outside the stated interval, choose the point at a right angle to the maximum direction. Its angle from the minimum direction is in [π/4,3π/4] as well. One of the two successive ratios via this point is at least the square root of the original ratio. Relabel the larger-image-distance point as a_j.

**A uniformly bilipschitz patch for each triple.** In normalized coordinates let z=0 and |a|=|b|=1. The angle condition allows the broken segment 0→a→b to be a graph in the direction of the unit bisector e of a and (b−a)/|b−a|. Their dot product is −sin(∠a0b/2), so both directions have projection on e at least sin(π/16). Rotate into the coordinates given by e. The broken segment is the graph of h on [0,e·b] with uniformly bounded slope and |h|≤1, and 0<e·a<e·b≤1. Extend h by zero to the left of 0 and linearly to zero at t=2, with zero to the right. The shear G(t,u)=(t,u+h(t)ρ(u)), where ρ(u)=max(0,1−|u|/2), is uniformly bilipschitz. For fixed t the second coordinate is an increasing onto map with lower Lipschitz bound 1/2. Comparing different t using the uniform slope bound gives the inverse Lipschitz estimate. G is the identity outside B(0,4). Write R for the rotation from the original horizontal direction to e, and write T for the radial twist in the original coordinates which equals R in B(0,4), tapers linearly in angle to the identity from radius 4 to 8 and fixes the exterior of B(0,8). The patch in the original coordinates is R ∘ G ∘ R⁻¹ ∘ T. The twist and inverse are uniformly Lipschitz by comparing rotations at the smaller radius and bounding the angle derivative. The composition is uniformly bilipschitz and fixes the exterior of B(0,8). On the horizontal segment with parameter 0≤t≤e·b, T acts by R and therefore the patch is precisely t↦R(t,h(t)). In particular it takes the horizontal diameter to an arc containing 0,a,b in that order. Rescale and translate this patch for each j.

**One essential witness curve.** Choose a smooth embedded circle whose intersection with a sufficiently small rectangle about zero is a straight horizontal arc across the rectangle, and keep a point w on it outside the rectangle. For the essential test in positive genus choose a smooth essential circle, place a point and tangent direction by smooth ambient isotopies, straighten its local graph by a smooth supported shear, and restrict to a small rectangle containing no other portion of that circle. If testing all quasicircles, a contractible smooth circle with the same local geometry suffices, including on the sphere. After discarding finitely many centers, replace the horizontal arc in the rectangle by a graph whose height is y_j on each interval [t_j−8r_j,t_j+8r_j]. The intervals are disjoint. Interpolate linearly between plateaus and to height zero at the positive end of the replacement. Set the height at 0 and to its left to zero. The geometric separation of t_j and |y_j|≤t_j/4 bound all slopes uniformly, including Lipschitz continuity at zero. Choose the rectangle and first retained index so that the whole graph is away from its top and bottom edges. The graph is isotopic to the straight arc relative to its endpoints, for example by multiplying its height by a parameter. Thus the new circle stays in the same isotopy class and is a quasicircle by local bounded turning for Lipschitz graphs and by compactness. It crosses each B(z_j,8r_j) along the horizontal diameter.

Apply the patches on their disjoint balls and the identity off the balls. The pasted map is bilipschitz in the chart. For points in distinct balls, split their straight segment at their balls’ boundaries. Since those boundary points are fixed, the triangle inequality gives the common Lipschitz bound. The same reasoning with one split works if one point lies off the balls. The same argument for the inverse yields a lower bound. The maps preserve each ball, and the union and its limit are inside the chart, so this extends by the identity to the surface. The resulting quasicircle C contains z_j,a_j,b_j in that order on the diameter replacement subarc, and w lies outside that subarc.

The two arcs of f(C) between f(z_j) and f(b_j) have diameter at least |F(a_j)−F(z_j)| in local coordinates up to a fixed factor on the first arc, and bounded below by a positive constant on the complementary arc using f(w). The local Euclidean and smooth surface distances are comparable near f(z_*). Both diameter-to-endpoint-distance ratios diverge, since z_j,b_j→z_* and the separated distortion ratios diverge. The estimate for the complementary subarc uses that its closure also contains f(z_j), and the diameter of an arc equals the diameter of its closure. Thus f(C) fails bounded turning, a contradiction.

## Graph corollary

For a closed orientable surface of genus at least two, every automorphism of the essential quasicircle graph QC†(S) is induced by a homeomorphism by the first assertion of the cited [Theorem A, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09892v1#page=2). That homeomorphism sends every essential quasicircle to a quasicircle and so is quasiconformal by this theorem. The canonical action of Q(S) is already known to be faithful in Theorem A. Consequently Q(S)→Aut QC†(S) is an isomorphism; every automorphism is coarsely order preserving in the authors' sense. No separate graph reconstruction is asserted here.
